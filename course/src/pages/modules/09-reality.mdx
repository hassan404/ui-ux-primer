This one is about the moment to distrust it.

## Why

Your judgement predicts users; it does not replace them. The strongest habit in this module fits in one sentence: watch a real person attempt a real task, and trust what they do over what anyone says, including you. Interviews, surveys, and star ratings report opinions, and opinions are polite, misremembered, and shaped by the question. Behavior is none of those. Every research method you will meet is a variation on getting closer to behavior. It is the nearest thing this field has to a consensus.

The reason this module is short: the cheap version of watching captures most of the value, and the expensive version mostly adds confidence, schedule, and a report nobody reads. You are not running a study. You are borrowing someone's fresh eyes for twenty minutes, monthly.

## The move

**The monthly twenty minutes.** One person who has never seen your product; a colleague from another team or a friend counts. One real task, phrased as a goal: "get a refund for the March order," never "click the billing tab." Ask them to think out loud, and then say nothing yourself, which is the hard part and most of the skill. No helping, no "it's actually up there," no defending. Write down where they hesitate, where they backtrack, what they say the interface is doing when it is doing something else. The moment they struggle with a thing you built, the urge to explain arrives with real force. Every explanation you swallow becomes a fix you understand.

**Score it with one question.** After each task: "Overall, how difficult was that, 1 to 7?" That single-ease question tracks over time, costs nothing, and correlates well with the heavyweight instruments. One number per task, watched across months, tells you whether the product is getting easier. The scale is validated in the research literature; it is not a vibe.

**The five-minute accessibility pass**, monthly, same cadence:

1. Unplug your mouse and complete your core task by keyboard. Tab order sane, focus always visible, nothing unreachable.
2. Run your key screens through a contrast checker; module 4's floor applies.
3. View the same screens in grayscale. Meaning that vanishes was riding on color alone. Try it on a card you know:

## Before you ship

That was the course. The regression tests, gathered:

- One stranger, one real task, thinking aloud, every month. Silence while they struggle.
- One difficulty score per task, tracked over time.
- Keyboard, contrast, grayscale, five minutes, same cadence.
- The critique card weekly; copywork monthly; the whys in writing.
- Spacing first when something looks off. The scale, the levers, the states. You know the checks now.

The course ends with a test you cannot cram for. [The exit drill](../../exit/) serves fourteen fresh discrimination trials, several on surfaces no module showed you, and scores your eye by family, so "I can see it now" becomes something you checked rather than something you read. [The checklist](../../checklist/) follows it: the whole course folded onto one printable page.

The eye keeps training after the course ends; that was the design. Ship something this week and run the twenty minutes on it.
