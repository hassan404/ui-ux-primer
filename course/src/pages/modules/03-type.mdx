## notice

Count the pixels on any screen you have shipped. Text covers most of them. Whatever your typography is doing, your interface is doing. Start with sizes:

<ABFlicker demo="typescale" client:only="react" />

## why

Module 1 gave you size as a hierarchy lever. Type is where the lever needs machinery, because a screen holds many sizes at once and your eye ranks them relative to each other. Sizes 1px apart refuse to rank; the reader sees one mumbling register. Sizes from a scale (each step noticeably larger, a ratio like 1.25 apart) rank instantly. Pick from the scale, never from between its steps. This is the spacing-scale discipline applied to text. <span class="conf">strong convention</span>

Two more variables shape how text reads, and they trade off against each other. Feel each one move:

<StepSlider demo="lineheight" client:visible />

<StepSlider demo="measure" client:visible />

The two interact: wider columns need taller line height, since the eye's return sweep travels farther. Set them together, never one at a time.

## the move

A working default kit, before taste enters:

- **Body at 16 or 17px.** Smaller belongs to captions and metadata, and stops at about 13px.
- **Line height near 1.5 for body text, near 1.2 for headings.** Large text needs less lead.
- **Prose columns capped at 45 to 75 characters.** Interfaces mostly hold short strings; the cap protects the paragraphs you do have.
- **Two font families at most**, and one works fine: the system stack costs nothing and offends no one. Spend your novelty budget elsewhere.
- **Weights at 400 and up.** Ultralight text below display sizes is decoration at readability's expense. Prefer a weight step over a size step for same-size emphasis.
- **Tabular figures for columns of numbers** (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`), so digits align and totals scan.

<span class="hatch">The 1.5 line height figure is a screen convention; book typographers set 1.2 to 1.45 and have for centuries. The regimes differ: immersive reading at close focal distance versus scanning at arm's length. WCAG asks for 1.5 on body text, which settles it for interfaces. Print rules stay right, in print. <span class="conf">sources conflict; taught as a judgment call</span></span>

## the tool

The two-size test. On any screen you own, count the distinct text sizes. Interfaces need fewer than you expect: one for content, one for metadata covers most cards and rows, with headings above. Past five sizes on one screen, some pair differs by a pixel or two, and that pair is where the mumble lives. Merge them and watch the screen firm up.

## before you ship

- Sizes come from the scale. No orphan sizes a step apart.
- Body text: 16px and up, line height near 1.5, columns inside 75 characters.
- One family, two weights, 400 minimum, covers most products.
- Numbers meant to be compared sit in tabular figures.
