## notice

Shadows carry physics. Your eye audits them whether you asked it to or not:

<ABFlicker demo="shadows" client:only="react" />

## why

You grew up under one sun. Your visual system assumes light arrives from above, and it reads shadows as elevation data: small sharp shadow, slightly raised; large soft shadow, floating closer to you. <span class="conf">replicated perception research</span> An interface that respects the assumption gets depth for free. An interface with a glow here and a sideways shadow there is claiming three suns, and the claim reads as sloppiness even to users who could never articulate it.

The deeper pattern, and it runs through this whole module: polish is consistency in the small decisions. Shadows, corner radii, border weights, icon sizes. Each individual choice is nearly arbitrary. Making the same choice everywhere is what looks professional. You have seen this before in the spacing scale; it is the same idea wearing different clothes.

## the move

**Shadows: two tiers, one light source.** A small tier for resting cards and inputs, a large tier for floating things like menus and dialogs. Both offset downward, never sideways. Define them once as tokens and stop deciding. This site runs on exactly two.

**Separation: space, then background, then border.** Borders are the loudest way to divide content and the first tool developers reach for. Reverse the order:

<BeforeAfter demo="separation" client:visible />

**Radius: one family, and nested corners shrink.** Pick a base radius and derive the rest. When a rounded element sits inside a rounded container, the inner radius must be smaller (outer minus the gap between them), or the corners visibly fight. Concentric corners are one of those details nobody names and everybody's eye checks.

**Icons: one size grid, one stroke weight, never scaled up.** Icons drawn for 24px turn balloon-limbed at 48. If you need a big icon, put the small one in a colored shape and let the shape carry the size.

**Optical beats mathematical.** A play triangle centered by geometry sits visibly off-center; text next to an icon needs a nudge; a circle must overshoot the cap height of text beside it to look equal. When math and eye disagree, the eye is right, because the eye is the instrument your users bring. <span class="conf">working craft consensus</span>

<span class="hatch">Dark interfaces change the shadow rules: shadows need darkness to read, and a dark UI has none to spare, so elevation shifts to surface lightness (higher surfaces render lighter). Flat design systems drop shadows on purpose and leave hierarchy to color and space; that is a coherent choice, applied consistently, which is the actual rule.</span>

## the tool

The consistency grep, run for real: search your stylesheets for `box-shadow`, `border-radius`, and `border:`. Count the distinct values. A polished codebase yields a handful of each; a drifted one yields dozens. Every value past the first few is a place your UI quietly disagrees with itself. Tokenize the survivors, delete the rest, and the interface tightens without a single layout change.

## before you ship

- Two shadow tiers, downward offsets, defined as tokens.
- Space first, background second, borders last.
- One radius family; inner corners smaller than outer.
- Icons on their grid, in shapes when large.
- When the eye and the ruler disagree, trust the eye.
