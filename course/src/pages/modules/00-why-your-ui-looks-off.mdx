## the repro

You know this moment. The feature works. The tests pass. You deploy the thing, open it in a clean tab to admire it — and something quietly dies in your chest. It's... sad-looking. You can't say *what's* wrong. You just know that if a stranger saw it, you'd want to explain that you're a good engineer, honestly.

So you do what any of us does with a mystery bug: you poke at it. A border here. A shadow there. Maybe an animation. It gets busier and somehow *worse*, and you close the laptop and tell yourself design is a gift you didn't get.

That diagnosis is wrong, and the rest of this course is the correct one.

## the root cause

Here's the part nobody tells you: **the bad feeling is the skill.** You *detected* the bug. Your eye — the part of design people claim you're missing — fired perfectly. What's missing is the debugger: the ability to read *why* it looks wrong and patch it. Detection before diagnosis, diagnosis before fix. You're not talentless; you're at step one of three, with the hard part — caring about the difference — already installed.

Ira Glass called this the taste gap: for years, your taste outruns your ability, and the gap feels like proof you're not meant for this. It's the opposite — the gap is the *entry requirement*. <span class="conf">strong consensus</span>

The second wrong belief is that design starts with a blank page and inspiration. It doesn't. Watch someone who's good: before any screen exists, they've pre-decided a handful of variables — a spacing scale, a type scale, one accent color and its shades — and then they apply them with boring, ruthless consistency. Design is not painting. Design is **declaring your constants, then refusing to hardcode magic numbers.** You already know how to do that. It's the most developer-shaped skill in the world, wearing an art-school jacket.

## the first suspect

When a UI "looks off" and you can't say why, it's usually not the colors and it's not your font. The stack trace almost always ends in the same frame: **spacing**. Gaps that are *almost* related. Padding eyeballed per-container. Nineteen pixels here because it looked fine at the time. Each decision was reasonable; together they read as noise — and noise is what "amateur" looks like. <span class="conf">the community's #1 diagnosis</span>

## how this course works

Every module is a bug report: **repro** (you'll recognize the failure), **root cause** (why it happens), **fix** (moves you can apply today), **regression test** (a checklist so it stays fixed). You'll be asked to commit to answers before reveals — guessing wrong in here is the point of here.

Three promises, since trust is a UX feature:

- **It's finite.** Ten modules, about three hours. No streaks, no login, nothing to maintain. Finishing is a feature, so the course is built to be finished.
- **Rules come with escape hatches.** Every rule here is a 90% rule, and the exceptions ship in the same breath — in boxes like the one above.
- **Claims carry their confidence.** Replicated science, strong convention, and folk wisdom will be labeled as what they are — like <span class="conf">this</span>.

## the regression test

- The "something's off" feeling is a **signal, not a verdict**. When it fires, say thanks — your detector works.
- When it fires, check **spacing first**. It's the usual suspect.
- Catch yourself **adding** things to fix ugliness. The fix is nearly always subtraction or alignment, not decoration.
- You are not behind. You're a developer with a working eye and no debugger — and the debugger is teachable, starting with the biggest lens there is: where the eye lands first.
